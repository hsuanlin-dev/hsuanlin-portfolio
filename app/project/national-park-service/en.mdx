# National Park Service — Scalable UI Framework

![image](/nps-en.png)
> **Project Link:** [https://www.nps.gov.tw/en](https://www.nps.gov.tw/en) ↗

## Overview

The National Park Service's public portal was built around a React component library based on the government's official UI guidelines. It follows atomic design principles, with colors, spacing, and other visual properties defined as design tokens.
Using the existing portal as the design reference, I independently rebuilt the frontends for two other government systems: the Taiwan Hiking Permit One-Stop Service Portal and an internal tax-related business system for the National Park Service under the Ministry of the Interior.

## Challenge

The three systems were completely independent, with separate repositories and different development histories.
The real challenge was making two very different legacy frontends look and behave as though they were built from the same design system as the National Park Service's public portal—without having access to its source code or any formal documentation.
I had to reverse-engineer the design and implementation patterns from the existing product itself, then apply those patterns consistently to completely separate codebases.

## What I Did

- Studied the existing portal's components and UI patterns in detail, reverse-engineering its development conventions without access to formal documentation.
- Completely rebuilt the frontend of the Taiwan Hiking Permit One-Stop Service Portal, replicating the same component patterns and design tokens.
- Applied the same approach to the internal tax-related business system for the National Park Service under the Ministry of the Interior, independently rebuilding the frontend and aligning it with the same visual standards.

## Outcome

Three independently developed government platforms now share a consistent visual language and interaction experience, despite having separate codebases and development histories.
