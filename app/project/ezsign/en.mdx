## Overview

I was responsible for the frontend visual and interaction implementation of EZSIGN, a B2B electronic signature platform, including its multi-step Wizard onboarding flow and landing page. In addition to the frontend implementation, I also built the platform's brand identity from scratch, including the logo, which was later officially registered as a trademark, and the overall color system. The underlying business logic and functionality were handled by other engineers on the team.

## Challenge

An electronic signing workflow cannot simply be reduced to a single form. Users need to upload documents, configure one or more signers and their signing order, and set notification preferences.
The core UX challenge was breaking these interconnected tasks into a clear, step-by-step workflow without overwhelming users with too much information at once.
The technical constraints were also predetermined—the project already used Bootstrap, so replacing the frontend framework was not an option. My role was to establish a new visual language within the existing architecture, using color, component styling, and visual hierarchy to make a legally oriented workflow involving sensitive documents feel trustworthy and professional.

## What I Did

- Designed and implemented a multi-step Wizard workflow, guiding users through basic information, document uploads, signer configuration, and notification settings while reducing the cognitive load of completing the process.
- Built the platform's brand identity from scratch, including the logo, which was later officially registered as a trademark, and the overall color system. Reworked the visual design within the existing Bootstrap architecture.
- Designed and implemented the platform's landing page, helping visitors quickly understand what EZSIGN does, how it fits their use case, and why they should choose Trade-Van Information Services.

## Outcome

The Wizard onboarding flow, brand identity, and redesigned interface were all launched as part of the EZSIGN B2B onboarding experience. The logo I designed was also officially registered as a trademark, becoming part of the company's brand assets.
